Showcase · D3 · robot dog

A robot dog, designed by an AI agent in Polykrom, that walks.

The third rung of the ladder: a quadruped with twelve servo joints, from an empty design. An AI agent (Claude Opus 5.5) built it in six stages with nothing but Polykrom's tools: the body, one leg, then four, a standing pose found by inverse kinematics and a crawl gait keyed on the twelve servos. It checked in Polykrom that the dog stands and keeps its balance through every step of the gait. Then the design went to MuJoCo, an independent physics engine, with nothing holding the body up. It stands, and it walks.

MuJoCo 3, running the MJCF Polykrom exported, with the base free. Polykrom's Crawl study is replayed as the twelve servos' targets, 0.05 s per step, three cycles. Nothing holds the body up: physics decides whether it walks.

How it was built

An AI agent (Claude Opus 5.5) running headless, with Polykrom's MCP tools as its only tools, built the dog in six stages, each picking up the design the stage before had saved. Times, costs and tool calls are from the agent's run records.

Measured, not claimed

The agent checked the dog with Polykrom's statics: the four feet as the support, the ground at z = 0, the servos' rated stall torque of 1.912 N·m. Then MuJoCo, a physics engine that knows nothing of Polykrom, ran the MJCF the agent exported.

In Polykrom

  • verifiedIt stands: the centre of mass is at (0.01, 0.00, 123.5) mm, over the polygon of the four feet, 114.7 mm inside its nearest edge.
  • verifiedServo loads standing: gravity puts 0.288 N·m on the knees (15.1 % of stall), 0.202 N·m on the abduction joints (10.6 %) and 0.058 N·m on the hips (3.0 %).
  • verifiedBalance through the crawl: one foot at a time (rear left, front left, rear right, front right), each lifted 30 mm and moved 40 mm, the body shifted over the other three feet before each lift. The smallest stability margin over the whole gait is 20.77 mm, against the 10 mm required.
  • verifiedServo loads walking: the hardest-working joint is the rear right knee at 0.596 N·m, 31 % of stall.
  • verifiedNo collisions: no interference in any of the gait's 161 samples.
  • verifiedPlanted feet stay put: the feet on the ground follow their paths within 0.04 mm.

In MuJoCo 3

  • verifiedIt stands: 2 s simulated with the base free; all twelve servos hold within 0.02° of their targets at their rated force limit (±1.912 N·m). No NaN, no warnings.
  • verifiedIt walks: the Crawl study replayed as servo targets, 0.05 s per step, three cycles, 24.5 s simulated. The dog walks 128.5 mm forward, 42.8 mm per cycle against the 40 mm designed, drifts 0.1 mm sideways, and its body never tilts more than 0.11°.

Where Polykrom fell short

A walking machine finds what a printer didn't. These turned up during the run; the last three are open issues being fixed.

Free-floating base

Polykrom's gait study can't move the body.

A motion study can't move a free-floating base, so in the Crawl study, its video and the 3D view above, the body stays still and the feet move. Polykrom's statics showed the dog keeps its balance at every step, but only MuJoCo, with the body free, shows that it really walks.

Render

The render is a preview.

The agent's render is preview quality: no shadows, a flat studio.

Driven joints

Driving a joint could reshape a part.

A part cut from another component could change shape when a joint moved. → #168, being fixed in #173

Speed

Small edits re-evaluated the whole assembly.

Renames and resizes re-evaluated the whole assembly: 79 of the run's 124 minutes were spent inside Polykrom's tools. → #169

Servo horns

Servo horns were fused to the case.

A library servo's horn was part of its case, so it couldn't turn with its joint. In the stand stage the agent split each of the twelve horns into a part of its own. → #170
